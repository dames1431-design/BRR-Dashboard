# 02 — Offer: "Score to Tuesday's Lesson"

*Offer Architect run 2026-11-15. Input: 01-market-signal.md (verdict: moderate-to-strong; no pivot needed). Idea: 14-minute BRR Dashboard walkthrough for K–5 literacy coaches.*

## The one urgent problem

In the coach's words: "We ran the universal screener. Half my second graders came back *below benchmark*. I have a composite score, a flexible group, and a PLC data meeting Thursday — and still no answer to *what do I teach tomorrow*. The kids we drilled on phonics decode now but can't tell me what happened in the story. And most of my English learners are flagged and I can't tell if it's reading risk or a language-exposure flag."

The scout quantified this: only 44% of screener-giving teachers said their school has a formal process for turning results into intervention plans (MIT survey, figures via secondary coverage). Screening has become a compliance exercise. The winter benchmark window (Dec–Feb) is when the pain peaks, and post-ESSER there are fewer people to act on the same data.

## Unique mechanism

**Two-sentence version (for a principal):** Phonics programs teach children to turn letters into sounds, and the screeners you already own measure how fast that happens — but neither tells a teacher what a child does with the words once they are decoded. Beyond Phonics adds that missing layer: the BRR Dashboard reads the screener scores you already have, locates the weakest of five language domains and six meaning categories, and turns that into a 12-minute lesson for tomorrow, so the data meeting is spent on instruction instead of interpretation.

**Fuller version:** Every competitor in the scout's table stops at a skill group or a Lexile band. BRR is decision-support that sits *on top of* mCLASS, i-Ready, or Star — not a replacement screener and not an assessment. It combines the composite and words-correct-per-minute the coach already has with attendance, work completion, and time on task, then adds two diagnostic lenses no screener offers: the five language domains (Phonology, Morphology, Syntax, Semantics, Pragmatics) and the six meaning categories (Object, Action, Property, State, Event, Relationship). The output is a Low/Moderate/High risk flag ("an at-risk flag, not a diagnosis"), the two weakest areas, and a ranked action list drawn from Beyond Phonics: STQ prompts tied to the read-aloud, a 10–12-minute domain mini-lesson, a real book at Lexile ±75 with a clear event chain, Orbit mapping for spiral review, Passcode Reasoning sheets feeding UCB bins, and a caregiver script for High-risk students. For multilingual learners, the domain view helps a coach separate "weak in English syntax/semantics" from "weak decoder" — the question Ohio and Massachusetts data say English-only screeners cannot answer. This is the comprehension layer the 2026 SRI and fade-out research gives districts permission to add without admitting their phonics purchase was wrong.

## Believability / proof plan

- **Lead magnet:** the demo *is* the proof — a composite everyone recognizes goes in, a printed lesson comes out in 14 minutes. Needs nothing invented.
- **Playbook:** credibility rests on the owner's Beyond Phonics authorship and the published research base behind it. **Flag:** the Content Strategist needs the owner's actual citations for the five-domain and six-category framework; do not substitute paraphrased "science of reading" claims.
- **Cohort:** proof is the routine itself plus a coach-facing sample PLC agenda. Reuse the scout's MIT (44%), SRI (two-thirds surface-level comprehension), and Ohio (>80% of ELs flagged) figures with their caveats; the MIT page was not retrieved directly.
- **Pilot:** the BRR risk score has **no published validation**. Every pilot document says "prototype," "research-informed decision-support." The evidence memo reports adoption and workflow changes, never "predicted dyslexia" or "closed the gap." **Flag for research:** a validation study design is a prerequisite for any claim beyond decision-support.

## Pricing rationale

The scout found coaches already pay $7–$14/student/year for screener bundles and $34.25/student/subject for i-Ready, so a per-student fourth dashboard is a hard sell in a post-ESSER budget. The ladder therefore prices the *method* where the market already spends: $47 sits between the UFLI manual (~$70) and a free state curriculum, cheap enough for a coach's own card; $497 matches a typical PD-day registration and comes in far under Heggerty Bridge ($1,479); the $6,500 building pilot is a flat fee, not per-student, so it can be defended against "$14/student already covers this" and lands inside the brief's $3k–$25k license band. Low rungs fund content; the pilot is where margin lives.
