### Email 2 — Day 2: Teach

**Subject:** Decodes every word. Can't say what happened.
**Alternate subject:** Why "weak comprehension" is usually one category
**Preview:** A three-minute check you can run during tomorrow's read-aloud.

Which cell did you circle? Reply with the category, one word. No student names, please. I read every answer and it shapes what I write next.

Here is why the cell matters. Word recognition and meaning construction are different jobs. Phonics teaches the first; the screener measures how fast it happens. Neither tells you what the child does with the words once they are decoded. That is why a second grader can read a paragraph aloud perfectly and tell you nothing about its sequence.

A way to find the category with no tool, inside the read-aloud you already give:

1. Stop after a short stretch with a clear chain of events.
2. Ask three STQ prompts in order: *What happened? Then what? Why did that happen?*
3. Note where the answer thins. A child who names the characters and objects but loses the order is breaking down in Event. A child who holds the order but misses *why* is often breaking down in Relationship.

That is the grid in three minutes, and it tells you which 12-minute lesson comes next.
