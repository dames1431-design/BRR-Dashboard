### Email 2 — Day 2: Teach
**Subject:** Decodes every word. Can't say what happened.
**Alternate:** Why "weak comprehension" is usually one category
**Preview:** A three-minute check you can run during tomorrow's read-aloud.

Which cell did you circle? Reply with one word. I read every answer and it shapes what I write next.

Here is why the cell matters. Word recognition and meaning construction are different jobs. Phonics teaches the first; the screener measures how fast it happens. Neither tells you what the child does with the words once they are decoded. That is why a second grader can read a paragraph aloud perfectly and tell you nothing about its sequence.

A way to find the category with no tool, inside the read-aloud you already give:

1. Stop after a short stretch with a clear chain of events.
2. Ask three STQ prompts in order: *What happened? Then what? Why did that happen?*
3. Note where the answer thins. A child who names characters and objects but loses the order is an Event child. One who holds the order but misses *why* is often Relationship.

That is the grid in three minutes, and it tells you which 12-minute lesson comes next.

### Email 3 — Day 4: Mechanism
**Subject:** Why 12 minutes and one domain, not a comprehension unit
**Alternate:** The mechanism behind Tuesday's lesson
**Preview:** One domain, one category, a real book at Lexile ±75, and a spiral.

Most comprehension instruction fails because it is general. The SRI observations cited in the walkthrough found roughly two-thirds of comprehension lessons were surface-level (their figure, their caveats). A broad strategy lesson spreads twelve minutes across six categories and lands on none.

Beyond Phonics inverts that:

- **One domain, one category.** Student A's lesson is Semantics × Event, so every minute is about the meaning of events, in order.
- **A real book at Lexile ±75,** chosen because its event chain is clear. The child can decode it; the work is holding what happened.
- **STQ prompts before the text,** so the child reads to answer a question, not to finish.
- **Orbit mapping** spirals the other five categories across the weeks between benchmarks.
- **Passcode Reasoning** sorts the child's own reasons into UCB bins and ends in a one-sentence principle the child can say.

The Grid gives you one such lesson. The Playbook has ten (two per domain) plus the STQ bank and Orbit template: [link]
